### 1\. Target Standard & Scope

*   **Standard & level:** WCAG 2.1 Level AA, adopted as a SELF-IMPOSED standard. Nobody is requiring it. It is adopted because it is the only complete, testable definition of "usable" that exists, and because writing it down now is cheaper than retrofitting it.
*   **Legal position:** no statutory obligation applies to a single-operator personal app. Nothing here is claimed as legal compliance. If a `FUTURE-STATE` commercial multi-tenant release happens, this page becomes the compliance baseline and 13.12 becomes real.
*   **Scope:** the React Native (Expo) mobile app, all hubs, all states. The `FUTURE-STATE` web companion inherits everything here plus §9.
*   **Out of scope:** printed/exported artifacts, third-party OS surfaces (system share sheet, biometric prompt chrome).
*   **Conformance goal:** every slice ships meeting §12 per-slice checks. Full-surface conformance is measured once, at the Phase 4 audit, on page 7.9.

### 2\. The Honest Note — Why This Page Exists With One Known User

There is one v1 user and she has no known disability. Writing an accessibility standard for an audience of one looks like ceremony. It is not, for two reasons.

**One — the use context IS an accessibility context.** The app is used in short bursts, early morning, frequently one-handed, sometimes while doing something else, often in low light before the eyes have adjusted. That is degraded vision, degraded attention, and one available thumb. Those are the same constraints accessibility work solves for, arriving by a different route. A layout that survives a screen reader and a 200% text scale is a layout that survives 6am.

**Two — the component API is written once.** Labels, target tokens, focus handling and reduce-motion branches are cheap to build in and expensive to retrofit across every component after the fact. The `FUTURE-STATE` commercial tenants and the real secondary users who already exist — the child profiles — are reason enough not to defer it.

**And the kill criterion cuts both ways.** The project stops and re-scopes if daily friction ends up higher than the Notion system it replaces. Accessibility failures ARE friction: a tap target that misses, a number that means nothing out of context, a Today list that clips at large text. This page is friction control that happens to also be accessibility.

### 4\. Colour & Contrast

This is a dark-base design running on UJG Color System v2.0. Dark bases fail differently from light ones: light-on-dark reads thinner than dark-on-light at the same computed ratio, and saturated accents on near-black look brighter than they compute. **Every pairing is computed. None is assumed.**

**Computed is not verified.** Every number in this section comes from the WCAG relative-luminance formula applied to the locked hex values. None of it has been confirmed on a physical display under a real colour profile. §12 is where computation becomes observation.

#### 4.1 Thresholds (WCAG 2.1 AA)

| Content | Minimum ratio |
| ---| --- |
| Body and small text — under 18pt regular, under 14pt bold | 4.5:1 |
| Large text — 18pt regular and up, 14pt bold and up | 3:1 |
| UI component boundaries, icons carrying meaning, focus rings | 3:1 |
| Chart series a reader must tell apart | 3:1 — graphical objects needed to understand content, so 1.4.11 reaches them |
| Decorative shapes, card fills that only group content, dividers, pure ornament | No requirement — see 4.5 |
| Disabled-state text | No conformance requirement — but the label must still be legible enough to read |

#### 4.5 Borders, and what elevation is actually made of

| Token | Value | Computed | Requirement | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| `border-meaningful` | Muted Platinum `#A8A5A0` at full value | 8.07 on Night | 3:1 under 1.4.11 | PASS with enormous headroom. Input outlines, toggle edges, unfilled button boundaries — every edge that tells you where a control is |
| `border-decorative` | Muted Platinum `#A8A5A0` at low opacity | Not applicable | None | Dividers and card edges. Carries no information, so it carries no obligation |

The split replaces a v1 token that tried to do both jobs at an unmeasured opacity and failed at the one that mattered.

**ELEVATION IS CARRIED BY BORDERS AND SPACING, NOT BY FILL LUMINANCE.** This is the design constraint the palette forces, and it is worth stating precisely because it was previously misrecorded as a WCAG failure.

*   A card fill that only groups related content is a DECORATIVE CONTAINER. It is not a user-interface component and it is not a graphical object needed to understand content. **1.4.11 does not reach it.** The content inside the card carries its own contrast, and that is what the criterion applies to. There is no ratio obligation on a card fill.
*   The measurement is still a real design fact: no green in this palette, tinted over Night, gets anywhere near 3:1. Midnight Forest at full value computes 1.35. Rich Forest at full value computes 2.52. A neutral lift would have to reach roughly `#5D5D5D` before a fill alone could carry the separation, and nothing that light belongs on this ground.
*   So the card component separates by border and spacing. That is decided once, here, and inherited everywhere. See 7.9 §4.5 for the corrected audit finding.

#### 4.6 The rule

**A colour pairing does not exist in the system until it has been computed and recorded in 4.3 or 4.4.** No pairing ships on the strength of "it looks fine on my screen."

*   New pairing → compute the ratio → add a row to 4.3 or 4.4 with its verdict → then it may be used, and only in the way its verdict allows.
*   Every pairing lives as a NAMED TOKEN PAIR in the Design System (4.5), not as two loose hex values a component picks independently. Components consume the pair, so an unmeasured combination is not reachable from component code.
*   **The thin-text rule.** Sunset Ember at 4.90 and the derived success text at 4.96 both clear AA and both sit under 5:1, which reads thinner than the number suggests on an OLED near-black. **Minimum 15pt and minimum medium weight when either is used as text.** This is a rule the ratio alone does not give.
*   **Muted and placeholder text uses Muted Platinum at full value, 8.07.** Fading a label by dropping its opacity until it looks right is banned. It was the app's highest AA risk under v1 precisely because the resulting value could not be computed, and v2 removed the need for it. Disabled state is the one exception and it is governed by `opacity-disabled`, still unverified — see §14.
*   **Retired v1 hexes are banned outright.** `#DCA424` · `#E86100` · `#5F2C82` · `#7E3209` · `#042D1D` do not appear anywhere in this app. A stale value pasted from an old file is the most likely way v1 gets back in, so they belong in the regression set on 7.11.

#### 4.7 Semantic ownership — one colour, one job

This is the rule v2 was built to enforce. Under v1 a single warm accent was both the error colour and a decorative book accent, which meant an error had no distinct visual signal and a person had to read the text to know something had gone wrong. Three jobs now sit on three colours.

| Job | Colour | Hex | Never used for |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Accent · do-now · focus ring | Luminous Gold | `#F2B01E` | Warning. Error. Success |
| Warning · overdue · stale · pending | Marigold | `#E28D1F` | Accent. Error. Destructive |
| Error · destructive | Sunset Ember | `#D9531A` | Anything decorative or routine. It appears when something is wrong or about to be destroyed, and at no other time |
| Success | Rich Jungle Green fill and icon, derived text | `#2E6B4F` and `#5B8972` | Accent. Any non-success state |

**GOLD AND MARIGOLD ARE SEVEN HUE DEGREES APART — 41 and 34.** Both clear contrast comfortably. The risk is perceptual, not numeric, and no ratio will catch it. Two mandatory mitigations, both binding on the component API:

1. **A warning always carries an icon AND a word.** Never the colour alone, at any size, in any component.
2. **Gold and Marigold never sit adjacent as the only distinction between two states.** If two things next to each other differ only by being gold or being marigold, the surface is wrong and gets redesigned.

Tracked as an open finding on 7.9 (A11Y-V2-003) until it has been looked at on a real screen during the personal beta.

#### 4.8 Colour is never the only carrier of meaning

Status, area and entity are the three things this app encodes with colour, and all three carry information that changes what the user does. **Each must survive a greyscale screenshot.**

| Encoded thing | Colour carries | Non-colour carrier — REQUIRED |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Action status | Accent fill or muted fill | A shape change — checkbox state — plus a text label in the detail view |
| Overdue vs due today | Marigold | An explicit text label reading Overdue, and position in the sort order |
| Area — Self · Home · Money · Business · Academic · Kids · Spirit | Per-area accent | A per-area icon AND the area name in text. The icon alone is not enough; seven icons are not learnable at a glance |
| Entity — Empire · UJG · JMR · Personal | Per-entity accent | The entity NAME rendered in the switcher and in the header at all times. See Interaction & Motion (4.12) |
| Pending offline write | Marigold with a muted treatment | A pending glyph plus the word Pending. See 4.12 |
| Error state | Sunset Ember | An icon plus the error text. Never a coloured border alone |
| Success confirmation | Rich Jungle Green fill, derived green text | An icon plus the confirming words. A green tick with no text is not a confirmation |
| Warning vs accent | Marigold vs Luminous Gold | An icon plus a word on the warning, per 4.7. This pair carries the heaviest load on this criterion in the whole system |
| Chart and rollup series | Series colour | A direct label on the series, or a value shown on tap. Never a colour-key legend alone. See 4.9 — the series set itself is an open blocking finding |

**Test:** screenshot the surface, desaturate it, and ask whether every decision is still makeable. If it is not, the surface fails.

| ---| ---| ---| ---| --- |

Whichever route is taken, the resulting series values get computed and recorded before any chart ships. Tracked as A11Y-V2-001, CRITICAL and BLOCKING.

### 5\. Touch Targets & Reach

The core interaction of this app is repeated tapping — quick capture, do-now, one-tap habit logging. **Target sizing is functional here, not cosmetic.** A 4% miss rate on a control tapped forty times a day is friction the kill criterion cares about.

| Rule | Value | Notes |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Minimum target size — all interactive elements | 44 × 44 pt | Exceeds the WCAG 2.1 AA 24 × 24 CSS px floor deliberately. Platform HIG, not the WCAG minimum, is the standard |
| Minimum target size — primary action targets | 48 × 48 pt | Quick capture, do-now, habit and routine log controls |
| Minimum spacing between adjacent targets | 8 pt | Measured edge to edge, not centre to centre |
| Minimum spacing — adjacent targets with different consequences | 16 pt | A log control must never sit 8pt from a delete control |
| Visual size may be smaller than target size | Yes | Extend the hit area with `hitslop`. A 24pt icon with a 44pt hit area is correct and expected |
| List row as a whole | Full row is the target | A row is tappable across its width; small controls inside it get their own 44pt targets and stop propagation |

**Tokenised so it cannot drift.** These are tokens in the Design System (4.5) — `target.min`, `target.primary`, `target.gap`, `target.gap-destructive`. No component hard-codes a size. A component that needs a size not in the token set is a design conversation, not a local override.

### 6\. Dynamic Type

**A Today list that breaks at large text sizes is broken.** Not degraded — broken. The Command Center is the surface the whole product is judged on, and it is the surface most likely to fail scaling because it ranks eleven proven surfaces into one phone column (see Information Architecture 4.1 §1.6).

| Rule | Requirement |
| ---| --- |
| Text scaling | All text respects the OS font scale. `allowfontscaling` is left at its default true; setting it false requires a written reason in the component file |
| Tested range | Every screen is checked at the default scale, at the largest standard scale, and at 200% |
| No clipping | No `numberoflines` truncation on any label that carries a decision. Truncation is permitted only on secondary descriptive text, and only with the full value reachable on tap |
| No hiding | The PRIMARY ACTION on a screen may never be the thing that scrolls off or collapses at large text. If something must give, secondary content gives first |
| Vertical growth | Rows grow in height. Fixed-height rows are banned wherever they contain scaled text |
| Layout switch | Horizontal arrangements — a label beside a value, a row of stat chips — reflow to vertical past a scale threshold rather than compressing. Read the scale with `pixelratio.getfontscale()` |
| Icon-plus-text pairs | Icons scale with the text they label, or the pairing breaks alignment at large scales |
| Minimum body size | 15pt at default scale. Nothing in the app is smaller than that before scaling. Error text and success text carry an additional weight floor — see 4.6 |

**The scaling failure mode to watch for:** a stat strip of four numbers across the top of the Command Center. At default scale it fits. At the largest scale it either clips, shrinks below the minimum, or pushes the Today list below the fold. All three are failures. The strip reflows or it ships as fewer stats.

### 7\. Screen Reader

| Rule | Requirement |
| ---| --- |
| Every interactive element is labelled | `accessibilitylabel` on every touchable. An unlabelled touchable does not pass review |
| Role is declared | `accessibilityrole` set correctly — button, link, header, checkbox, switch, adjustable |
| State is declared | `accessibilitystate` for selected, disabled, checked, expanded, busy |
| Decorative elements are hidden | Ornament and duplicate icons get `accessibilityelementshidden` / `importantforaccessibility` set to no, so the reader is not read a list of decorations |
| Grouping | A list row reads as ONE element with a composed label, not as five fragments the user must swipe through |
| Live regions | Toasts, queue-settled confirmations and errors announce via a live region. They do not steal focus |
| Reading order | Matches visual order. Verified by swiping the whole screen, not by inspecting code |
| Headings | Section headers carry the header role so the reader can jump between them |
| Semantic state is spoken, not coloured | A warning, an error, and a success are each announced in words. The three-colour split in 4.7 does nothing for a screen reader, so the word carries the whole signal |

#### 7.1 The do-now affordance must announce what it will do

The do-now deep link is the app's core action-first mechanic (Information Architecture 4.1 §7). **Announcing it as "button" is a failure.** The whole point of the control is that it goes somewhere specific, and a user who cannot see the row cannot infer where.

| Element | Announcement pattern |
| ---| --- |
| Do-now on an Action | Label names the action and the destination, in that order. Hint states the consequence — that it opens the place the Action gets completed |
| Do-now where the target is gone | Announced as unavailable, with the reason. Never a silent no-op. See Interaction & Motion (4.12) |
| Quick capture | Announced as capture, with the hint that it saves to the inbox |
| Habit and routine log control | Announced with the habit name and its current logged state, so a repeat tap is not made blind |
| Undo after a log | Announced as undo, naming what it will undo |

#### 7.2 Stat strip values need accessible labels

A bare number is meaningless out of context. A screen reader landing on "4" has been told nothing.

*   **Rule:** every rolled-up value on the Command Center exposes a composed label carrying WHAT the number is, WHAT ITS UNIT is, and WHAT PERIOD it covers. The visible label may be abbreviated; the accessible label may not.
*   Money values announce as currency, not as a bare figure.
*   A value that is a fraction of a whole announces both parts, not just the numerator.
*   A value still loading announces as loading, not as zero. **Announcing an unloaded number as zero is a correctness bug, not a cosmetic one** — see Content Design (4.9) on unknown and loading states.
*   Every computed value's provenance is reachable — the tap-through to what it was computed from is itself a labelled control. See 4.9.
*   A value in a warning or overdue state announces that state in words. Marigold is not audible.

### 8\. Motion

*   **Reduce Motion is respected everywhere.** Read once at app start and subscribed to for changes via `accessibilityinfo`. It is a value in a context provider, not a check each component makes for itself.
*   **Nothing essential is conveyed by motion alone.** If an animation is the only signal that a write landed, that write is unconfirmed for anyone with reduce-motion on. Every motion pattern has a static equivalent that carries the same information.
*   **No auto-playing motion.** Nothing loops, pulses, or animates without a user action that caused it. A perpetually pulsing badge is banned.
*   **No parallax, no motion-driven layout.** Scroll position does not drive decorative transforms.
*   **Nothing flashes.** No content flashes more than three times per second, at any point, under any state.

Full pattern-by-pattern specification, including the reduce-motion fallback for each, lives in Interaction & Motion (4.12) §Motion Patterns. **That page owns the patterns. This page owns the requirement that every one of them has a fallback.**

### 9\. Focus & Keyboard — `FUTURE-STATE`

The web companion does not exist. **This section is written now specifically so the component API does not have to be retrofitted**, because focus handling is the one accessibility concern that cannot be bolted on to an already-shipped component set. It touches every interactive component's props and every overlay's lifecycle.

| Requirement | Detail |
| ---| --- |
| Full keyboard operability | Every action reachable and completable without a pointer. No keyboard traps |
| Visible focus indicator | Luminous Gold `#F2B01E` — computed at 10.37 on Night and 10.02 on the raised surface, so it clears the 3:1 requirement with room. Any web surface value that is not one of those two gets its own computation and its own row in 4.3 before the web build starts |
| Focus order | Follows the visual and DOM order. The mobile reading-order work in §7 is the same ordering problem and produces the same answer |
| Focus on overlay open | Moves into the sheet or dialog and is trapped there |
| Focus on overlay close | Returns to the control that opened it. The component must retain the opener reference from day one — this is the specific retrofit this section prevents |
| Skip link | To main content, on every page |
| Escape | Closes any dismissible overlay |
| Biometric gate | Keyboard-operable fallback path required — see §11 |

**Carried into the mobile component API now:** every overlay component takes and stores its opener reference, every interactive component accepts an externally supplied focus ref, and every list row exposes a stable identity. None of that costs anything on mobile. All of it is expensive to add later.

### 10\. One-Handed Reach

The app is used one-handed in short bursts. Reach is treated as an accessibility requirement, not an ergonomic nicety — a control the thumb cannot reach is a control that requires a second hand the user does not have.

| Zone | What belongs there |
| ---| --- |
| THUMB ZONE — bottom third of the screen | The tab bar. Quick capture. The primary action on any screen. Sheet confirm buttons. Log and undo controls |
| MID ZONE — middle third | Content. Scrollable lists. Row-level do-now controls |
| STRETCH ZONE — top of the screen | Navigation-back. Titles. The entity and profile switcher. Settings entry points |
| DELIBERATELY OUT OF REACH | DESTRUCTIVE ACTIONS. Delete, permanent dismissal, and anything irreversible are placed in the stretch zone or behind a detail view — never adjacent to a frequently tapped control. These are the Sunset Ember controls, and the colour and the placement carry the same message |

**The reach rule and the spacing rule work together.** A destructive control 8pt from a log control is a mis-tap generator even if both are 44pt. §5 sets 16pt minimum between targets of differing consequence; this section sets the zone. Both apply.

**Sheets are bottom-anchored** so their controls land in the thumb zone by construction. A sheet with its confirm button at the top is wrong.

### 11\. Biometric Accessibility

A biometric gate renders nothing until it passes (Information Architecture 4.1 §7). That is correct for privacy and it is a single point of failure for access. **Biometrics fail routinely for reasons that have nothing to do with the user's identity** — wet hands, a mask, poor light, a hardware sensor that is unavailable, a device that never enrolled one, or an accessibility configuration where face authentication is impractical.

| Requirement | Detail |
| ---| --- |
| A fallback path MUST exist | Device passcode / credential fallback, invoked through the platform's own authentication flow |
| The fallback does not weaken the gate | It authenticates to the SAME standard and unlocks the SAME scope for the SAME duration. It is not a shortcut, a bypass, a remember-me, or a reduced-privilege view |
| No degraded content on fallback | Falling back does not produce a preview, a partial render, or a redacted version. The gate is binary: passed or nothing |
| Failure is explained | A failed or cancelled attempt states what happened and offers the fallback explicitly. It does not dump the user to a blank screen or a bare error code — see Content Design (4.9) |
| Cancel is a first-class outcome | Cancelling returns to the previous surface intact. It is not an error state |
| The gate control is fully labelled | Announced with what it protects, in neutral terms only. The label never names the content behind the gate — see 4.9 on sensitive content |
| Timing | No time limit on completing authentication that the user cannot extend |
| Keyboard path | Required when the `FUTURE-STATE` web companion exists — §9 |

**The failure this prevents:** a sensor that stops working locks the owner out of her own records with no route in. That is not a privacy feature, it is data loss with extra steps.

### 12\. Testing

Two tiers. Cheap checks run on every slice; expensive checks run once at the audit. **Splitting them is what keeps the per-slice cost low enough that the checks actually get run** — an accessibility process that adds an hour per slice gets skipped by a solo builder, and a skipped process is worth nothing.

#### 12.1 Per slice — every slice, no exceptions

| Check | Method | Pass criteria |
| ---| ---| --- |
| CONTRAST | Every new colour pairing computed and recorded in §4.3 or §4.4 before use | No unmeasured pairing exists in the slice. No banned pairing used. No retired v1 hex present anywhere |
| LABELS | Every touchable in the slice has a label, role, and state | Zero unlabelled touchables. Do-now and log controls announce their consequence, not just their type |
| TARGET SIZE | Token check — is every interactive element on a target token | No hard-coded sizes. No adjacent differing-consequence targets under 16pt apart |
| DYNAMIC TYPE | Open the screen at the largest standard scale and at 200% | No clipping. No hidden primary action. Nothing below the minimum body size |
| SEMANTIC OWNERSHIP | Search the slice for the semantic tokens | Gold is not doing a warning's job. Marigold is not doing an error's job. Ember appears only on error and destructive surfaces. Every warning has an icon and a word |
| Colour-alone | Desaturated screenshot of the new surface | Every decision still makeable. Gold and marigold states still tellable apart |
| Reduce Motion | Toggle the OS setting and re-run the slice's interactions | Every animation has a working static fallback. Nothing is unconfirmed |

Run by the builder, on device, before the slice is called done. **These five capitalised checks are the Definition of Done for accessibility.** A slice that has not had them run is not done, regardless of whether it works.

#### 12.2 At the Phase 4 audit — page 7.9

Page 7.9 holds the audit itself. This page defines the standard; 7.9 records the result of measuring the whole surface against it.

| Check | Why it waits for the audit |
| ---| --- |
| Full VoiceOver traversal of every screen, start to finish | Only meaningful once the flows connect end to end. Per-slice reader checks catch labels; only a full traversal catches reading ORDER and grouping across screens |
| TalkBack pass | Second-platform verification; expensive to run per slice |
| Full-surface contrast sweep against the shipped build | Catches pairings that arrived through composition rather than through a token — a coloured surface under a coloured label |
| Gold versus Marigold on a real display | The one check computation cannot perform. Seven hue degrees apart, both passing contrast, and only a human eye on a real screen can settle whether the mitigations in 4.7 are sufficient |
| Chart series legibility with real data | Blocked on the resolution of 4.9. No chart is audited until the series set conforms |
| Disabled-state perceptibility at 0.38 | A multiplier, not a hex — its result depends on what it is applied to, so it can only be judged on a rendered surface |
| Dynamic Type sweep at every scale on every screen | Per-slice checks cover the slice's screens. The audit covers interaction between screens, and the Command Center under a full data load |
| Reading order across navigation transitions | Requires the whole navigation graph |
| Keyboard and focus | `FUTURE-STATE` — only when the web companion exists |
| One-handed reach validation with real data volume | Needs realistic content density to be worth anything |
| Conformance statement against WCAG 2.1 AA, criterion by criterion | The single formal output. Feeds Compliance Statement (13.12) |

**Automated scanning:** no CI accessibility scanner is in place. Recorded honestly as `FUTURE-STATE` rather than aspirationally as a process. The per-slice checks in 12.1 are manual and take minutes, which is why they survive contact with a solo build.

**Usability testing with disabled users:** `PAUSED`. Not viable at solo scale with one known user. It is the correct thing to do before any commercial multi-tenant release and it is recorded here so that the gap is a known one rather than a forgotten one.

### 13\. Governance & Remediation

*   **Definition of Done:** the seven per-slice checks in 12.1, all passing, on device.
*   **Exceptions:** an exception is recorded as a row in §14 with a named reason and a revisit trigger. Undocumented exceptions do not exist — an unmeasured pairing or an unlabelled control is a defect, not an exception.
*   **Palette changes are governed.** A palette migration invalidates every ratio on this page. When the palette moves, §4 is recomputed in full and 7.9 is re-run before the next slice ships. The v1 to v2 migration on 2026-08-26 is the worked example.
*   **Solo-build reality:** the builder, reviewer and approver are the same person. The mitigation is that the checks are mechanical and produce artifacts — a ratio, a desaturated screenshot, a screen at 200% — that can be checked later by someone else, including future Omegea.

| Severity | Definition | Handling |
| ---| ---| --- |
| CRITICAL | Blocks a task entirely for an AT user, or locks a user out — a failed gate with no fallback, an unlabelled primary action, a chart series a reader cannot distinguish | Fix before the slice ships. Never carried |
| HIGH | Major barrier with a workaround — a banned pairing on body text, a primary action lost at large text | Fix in the slice, or record as an exception in §14 with a revisit trigger |
| MEDIUM / LOW | Degraded but usable — imperfect grouping, a secondary label that could read better | Logged and cleared at the Phase 4 audit |
