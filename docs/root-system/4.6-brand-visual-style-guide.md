### 1\. Brand Overview

*   **What this is:** the visual identity of the **Life OS App** — a personal operator tool. A mobile-first React Native app the owner uses every day, often at 6am, often one-handed.
*   **Essence:** Afro-Futurist. Dark base, gold accents, warmth and depth rather than flat corporate minimalism.
*   **Personality:** calm, deliberate, warm, a little ceremonial. Not clinical. Not playful.
*   **Phasing:** the Afro-Futurist pass is **Phase 4** — texture, depth and refinement land on components that already work. Phase 2 builds the system in its plain form (4.5). Ornament is never a prerequisite for function.

#### 1.1 Relationship to the parent brand

This app **inherits the palette and the spirit of Urban Jungle Goddess. It is not bound by marketing-brand rules.**

| Inherited | Not inherited |
| ---| --- |
| UJG Color System v2.0 — 6 core, 2 functional, 7 earth tones | Marketing layout conventions and web-page composition |
| The Afro-Futurist direction and its warmth | Client-facing brand voice — in-app copy follows 4.9, not sales copy |
| The palette's existing meaning, which informs semantic naming (§3.3) | Wordmark-forward branding — this app does not sell itself to the user who opens it |
| The four-font stack AS A STARTING POINT, not as a requirement (§4) | Print and social specifications |
| The Golden Rule — no more than three colours in one design element | The 20 trios, 40 gradients and 20 mood palettes — those are collateral tools, not UI tools |

**Why this matters:** the person opening this app is the person who owns the brand. She does not need to be marketed to. Anything that exists to communicate the brand rather than to move work forward is decoration, and §2 governs decoration.

### 2\. The Afro-Futurist Direction — Concretely

Direction words are useless without a rule attached. Each line below can be used to reject a design choice.

#### 2.1 What it means visually, here

| Trait | What it actually is in this app |
| ---| --- |
| DARK BASE | Night is the ground. Surfaces rise from it in small luminance steps, not by switching to grey. Elevation is felt, not outlined — except where a boundary carries meaning, and then it is Muted Platinum. See §3.5. |
| HIGH-CHROMA ACCENT, USED SPARINGLY | Luminous Gold is the app's voice. It marks the ONE thing on a screen that matters most — the do-now action, the active state. Two accents shouting on one screen means neither is an accent. |
| WARMTH OVER STERILITY | The neutrals lean warm. Platinum, not white. Muted Platinum, not grey. Midnight Forest in the surface family rather than blue-grey. The app should feel like a room, not a dashboard. |
| TEXTURE AND DEPTH | Subtle grain, layered translucency, deep shadow — depth that suggests material. `FUTURE-STATE` for Phase 4, and always behind content. |
| CEREMONY WHERE IT IS EARNED | Gated and reflective surfaces — spiritual practice, journaling — may carry more visual weight than a task list. Deep Amethyst is the fill that marks them. Ceremony belongs to the rare surface, not the daily one. |

#### 2.2 What it must NOT become

This is the harder half and the one that gets violated first.

*   **It must not become decoration that competes with legibility at 6am on a phone.** That is the whole constraint. A tired person, low light, one hand, trying to log a habit. Anything that makes that harder has failed, no matter how good it looks in a screenshot.
*   **No texture behind body text.** Grain over type destroys the contrast ratio it was tested at.
*   **No gradient behind an interactive control.** The forty gradients in Color System v2.0 are collateral tools. A control's contrast has to hold at every point under it, and a gradient means it does not.
*   **No accent used as ornament.** If Luminous Gold appears somewhere that is not actionable or active, it is teaching the eye to ignore Luminous Gold.
*   **No glow, bloom or neon-on-black.** Afro-Futurist is not sci-fi UI. Halation around type is a legibility failure.
*   **No dark-for-mood.** The base is dark because it is a night-and-early-morning tool, not because dark reads as premium. Where dark hurts a task, dark loses.
*   **No pattern that reads as noise at small size.** Anything ornamental must be legible or invisible — never almost-legible.
*   **No colour-only meaning.** See §3.6.

**The test:** would this survive being looked at for one second by someone who just woke up and wants to tap one button?

### 3\. Colour Palette — UJG Color System v2.0

**This section was rebuilt on 2026-08-26.** The app previously ran on the v1 seven-colour palette. Every v1 hex is now retired. The migration table lives in the Design & Metaphor Map page; this section is the app's own reading of v2.

#### 3.1 What changed and why it matters here

| v1 (RETIRED) | v2 (CURRENT) | Consequence for this app |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Goldenrod `#DCA424` | Luminous Gold `#F2B01E` | Primary accent gains headroom — 8.8:1 becomes 10.4:1 on Night |
| Spanish Orange `#E86100` | Sunset Ember `#D9531A` | Slightly less headroom — 5.8:1 becomes 4.9:1. Still passes AA on Night, with the least margin in the set. |
| Eminence `#5F2C82` | Deep Amethyst `#47107D` | Darker. Confirms it as a FILL, never a text or border colour. |
| Sienna `#7E3209` | Terracotta `#B04720` | Brighter — 2.2:1 becomes 3.6:1. Still fails as text, now usable as a non-text mark. |
| Dark Green `#042D1D` | Midnight Forest `#042F1E` | Same value, renamed. Still a ground, still 1.3:1. |
| — | Rich Forest `#0D5E39` | NEW. The green you can actually see — 2.5:1. |
| — | Muted Platinum `#A8A5A0` | NEW, and the most important addition. A DEFINED secondary-text colour at 8.1:1. |
| — | Rich Jungle Green `#2E6B4F` | NEW. A defined success colour at 3.1:1. |

**The two new neutrals are the whole story.** v1's biggest accessibility risk was that muted text and subtle borders were made by dropping opacity until it looked right — an unverifiable guess, flagged in 7.9 as the most likely AA failure in the app. v2 ships Muted Platinum as a real value. That risk is closed, not mitigated.

#### 3.2 The palette as this app uses it

The app adds no new colours. It uses a SUBSET of v2 and assigns roles.

| Name | Hex | Ratio on Night | Role in this app |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Night | `#0A0A0A` | — | Base background. The default state of every screen. |
| Platinum | `#E8E6E1` | 15.87 | Primary text. Every readable word defaults to this. |
| Muted Platinum | `#A8A5A0` | 8.07 | Secondary text AND meaning-carrying borders. A REAL VALUE, not an opacity. |
| Luminous Gold | `#F2B01E` | 10.37 | PRIMARY ACCENT. Do-now action, active state, focus ring. |
| Marigold | `#E28D1F` | 7.60 | WARNING. Overdue, pending write, stale data, attention-needed. |
| Sunset Ember | `#D9531A` | 4.90 | ERROR AND DESTRUCTIVE ONLY. Nothing else. |
| Rich Jungle Green | `#2E6B4F` | 3.14 | SUCCESS as a fill or icon. NOT as text — see §3.4. |
| Midnight Forest | `#042F1E` | 1.35 | Surface tint over Night. A ground, never a mark. |
| Rich Forest | `#0D5E39` | 2.52 | Identity fill — the green that reads. Not text, not a border. |
| Deep Amethyst | `#47107D` | 1.54 | Gated and reflective surface fill. Carries Platinum or Gold text ON it. Never text itself. |

Not used in the app UI: Clay Ember, Harvest Gold, Sage, Warm Sand, Terracotta. They belong to collateral and the Amazonia register, and adding them here would give the app more hues than it can distinguish.

#### 3.3 Semantic roles — and the collision, resolved

Components never reference the names above — they reference these roles, which are the semantic tokens defined in 4.7 §3.

| Semantic role | Palette source | Used for |
| ---| ---| --- |
| BACKGROUND | Night | The screen ground |
| SURFACE | Midnight Forest tinted over Night | Cards, list containers, tab bar |
| ELEVATED SURFACE | Midnight Forest, raised a step | Sheets, modals, quick-add |
| CEREMONIAL SURFACE | Deep Amethyst | Gated and reflective screens — journal, spiritual practice |
| TEXT PRIMARY | Platinum | Body, labels, values |
| TEXT MUTED | Muted Platinum AT FULL OPACITY | Metadata, timestamps, secondary labels |
| ACCENT PRIMARY | Luminous Gold | Do-now, active tab, focus ring, primary action |
| BORDER MEANINGFUL | Muted Platinum | Input outlines, toggle edges, anything whose boundary IS the affordance |
| BORDER DECORATIVE | Muted Platinum at low opacity | Dividers, card edges — grouping only, no AA obligation |
| SUCCESS | Rich Jungle Green fill or icon | Completed, logged, synced |
| SUCCESS TEXT | Rich Jungle Green lightened — `#5B8972` | The word "Synced", "Done", "Logged" when set as text |
| WARNING | Marigold | Overdue, pending write, stale data, attention-needed |
| ERROR | Sunset Ember | Failed write, load failure, invalid input, destructive confirm |
| FOCUS RING | Luminous Gold | Keyboard and assistive focus — never removed, never subtle |

**The v1 collision is closed.** In v1, Spanish Orange served as both ERROR and as the ship-and-close urgency accent — bad news and good urgency wearing the same colour. It is resolved by separating the three jobs onto three colours: Gold pulls forward, Marigold warns, Ember is the only colour that ever means something went wrong. "Ship and close" was never a UI state — it is a Book accent meaning, and it stays in the Design & Metaphor Map where it belongs.

**Residual risk, named honestly:** Luminous Gold (hue 41°) and Marigold (hue 34°) are close. Side by side, in a low-light room, at small size, a tired person may not separate them. **Mitigation is mandatory, not optional: a warning always carries an icon and a word. Gold and Marigold never sit adjacent as the only thing distinguishing two states.** This is tracked in §10.

#### 3.4 Contrast — computed against v2

**This is a dark-base design, and dark-base designs fail contrast quietly.** WCAG 2.1 AA: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and for non-text interface components.

Computed from the v2 hexes against Night `#0A0A0A`:

| Foreground on Night | Ratio | Verdict |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Platinum `#E8E6E1` | 15.87 | AAA. This is why body text is Platinum, always. |
| Luminous Gold `#F2B01E` | 10.37 | AAA. Comfortable headroom even on a raised surface (10.02). |
| Muted Platinum `#A8A5A0` | 8.07 | AAA. Holds at 7.79 on the surface tint. Closes the muted-text risk. |
| Marigold `#E28D1F` | 7.60 | AAA. Holds at 7.34 on surface. |
| Sunset Ember `#D9531A` | 4.90 | AA for normal text, with the LEAST headroom in the set. Drops to 4.74 on surface. Marginal at small sizes and thin weights. |
| Terracotta `#B04720` | 3.55 | FAILS as text. Large text and non-text marks only. |
| Rich Jungle Green `#2E6B4F` | 3.14 | FAILS as text. Fill and icon only — hence SUCCESS TEXT gets its own value. |
| Rich Forest `#0D5E39` | 2.52 | FAILS as text. Identity fill only. |
| Deep Amethyst `#47107D` | 1.54 | FAILS as text. Surface fill only. Carries Platinum (10.28) or Gold (6.72) ON it. |
| Midnight Forest `#042F1E` | 1.35 | FAILS by design. It is a ground, not a colour to write in. |

**Text placed ON an accent fill:**

| Fill | Night label | Platinum label | Use |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Luminous Gold `#F2B01E` | 10.37 | 1.53 | NIGHT. Never Platinum. |
| Marigold `#E28D1F` | 7.60 | 2.09 | NIGHT. Never Platinum. |
| Sunset Ember `#D9531A` | 4.90 | 3.24 | NIGHT. Platinum fails AA here. |
| Deep Amethyst `#47107D` | 1.54 | 10.28 | PLATINUM. Never Night. |
| Rich Jungle Green `#2E6B4F` | 3.14 | 5.05 | PLATINUM. |
| Rich Forest `#0D5E39` | 2.52 | 6.29 | PLATINUM. |

**Rule:** a warm fill takes Night text. A cool or dark fill takes Platinum text. There is no fill in this palette where both work, so the choice is never a preference.

#### 3.5 Surfaces cannot separate by fill, and that is fine

Measured: **no green in this palette, tinted over Night at any usable strength, reaches 3:1 against Night.** Midnight Forest at full strength is 1.35. Rich Forest at full strength is 2.52. A neutral lift of Night would have to reach roughly `#5D5D5D` to clear 3:1 — far outside anything that reads as a dark UI.

The v1 audit recorded this as a WCAG failure. **That reading was wrong, and it is corrected here.** WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for user-interface components and for graphical objects needed to understand content. A card background that groups related content is neither — it is a decorative container, and the content inside it carries its own contrast. There is no conformance obligation on the card fill.

What DOES carry the obligation, and how it is met:

| Element | Obligation | How it is met |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Input field outline | 3:1 — the boundary IS the affordance | Muted Platinum `#A8A5A0`, 8.07 on Night. Enormous headroom. |
| Toggle, checkbox, radio boundary and state | 3:1 | Muted Platinum for the boundary, Luminous Gold for the on-state |
| Button boundary, where the button has no fill | 3:1 | Muted Platinum |
| Focus indicator | 3:1 against both the component and the adjacent ground | Luminous Gold, 10.37 |
| Chart series a reader must tell apart | 3:1 against the plot background | NOT MET by the v2 chart set — see §10 |
| Card or list-container fill | none | Midnight Forest tint. Free choice. |
| Divider between rows | none | Muted Platinum at low opacity |

**The practical consequence:** elevation on this app is carried by BORDERS AND SPACING, not by fill luminance. Trying to make cards float by tinting them harder is chasing a step the palette cannot produce, and it will end with a muddy green ground and no more separation than it started with.

#### 3.6 Colour is never the only signal

Overdue, error, pending, selected and completed each carry text, icon or shape as well as colour. This covers colour-vision deficiency, it covers the Gold-versus-Marigold hue proximity in §3.3, and it covers the case where contrast is technically passing but perceptually weak at 6am.

`FUTURE-STATE` — a light theme. It does not exist at v2.0, and the semantic token layer in 4.7 is what makes it possible later without touching components. v2's Platinum Tech palette (Platinum · Muted Platinum · Amethyst · Night) is the intended starting point when that day comes.

### 4\. Typography — A Decision To Confirm, Not An Assumption

The parent brand runs a four-font system: **Methanerse** for display and wordmarks, **Alister** for a signature variant, **Urbanist** for body and utility, **Mallong** for organic ornamental display.

**Adopting that full stack in this app is a decision to confirm, not something to inherit by default.** Mobile app typography has to be decided against what actually loads and renders well in React Native at small sizes and under OS accessibility scaling. A face that is beautiful on a website header is not automatically a face that survives at 13 on a phone in a hinted, scaled, anti-aliased render — and every custom font added to an Expo bundle costs load time and app size.

#### 4.1 Where each face stands

| Face | Brand role | Status in this app | Why |
| ---| ---| ---| --- |
| Urbanist | Body and utility | THE SAFE DEFAULT for body, labels and all UI text | Designed as a UI-friendly geometric sans, wide weight range, well-behaved at small sizes. Lowest risk. |
| Methanerse | Display and wordmarks | DESIRABLE FOR HEADERS — must be validated first | A display face for screen titles and stat values would carry the brand properly. It has to be proven legible at real header sizes on device and cheap enough in bundle terms. Unvalidated. |
| Alister | Signature variant, 25% tracking | Not proposed for in-app use | Signature faces are for marks and moments, not for interfaces. Possible on a splash screen only. |
| Mallong | Organic ornamental display | `PAUSED` for this app | Ornamental display has no role in a tool measured in taps-to-log. Revisit in Phase 4 for a single ceremonial surface, if any. |

#### 4.2 What has to be true before the stack is locked

1. The face renders cleanly in React Native on both platforms at 12 through 16, in the weights actually used.
2. It survives OS accessibility text scaling at the largest setting without breaking layout or losing controls.
3. Its added bundle weight is measured, not estimated, and judged worth it.
4. Numerals are legible and ideally tabular — this app shows money and counts constantly, and jumping digits in a stat strip is a real defect.
5. A system-font fallback is defined for every face, so a font that fails to load degrades to something readable rather than something wrong.

**Until those are proven: Urbanist for everything, and a display face is an upgrade, not a foundation.**

#### 4.3 Type scale

Sizes, weights, line heights and letter spacing are tokenized in 4.7 §5. The scale is defined there; this page owns only the decision about which faces fill it.

| Style | Intended face | Usage |
| ---| ---| --- |
| Display | Methanerse if validated, else Urbanist | Rare — a screen's own name, a headline stat |
| Heading | Methanerse if validated, else Urbanist | Screen titles, section headers |
| Body | Urbanist | Everything readable |
| Label | Urbanist | Buttons, chips, tab bar, field labels |
| Numeric | Urbanist, tabular figures | Money, counts, timers, streaks |
| Caption | Urbanist | Metadata, timestamps — set in Muted Platinum, see §3.2 |

**Rule:** no more than two faces render on any one screen. A third face is a design failure, not variety.

### 5\. Iconography

*   **Style:** line, single consistent weight, rounded terminals to match the warmth of the palette. No mixing line and filled in the same context — filled is reserved for the ACTIVE state of an otherwise-line icon.
*   **Grid:** 24 nominal. 20 inline with text, 28 in the tab bar. Optical size, not just box size — an icon that reads smaller than its neighbours gets adjusted.
*   **Colour:** icons default to TEXT PRIMARY or TEXT MUTED. Accent colour on an icon means the icon is actionable or active, never that it is important.
*   **Touch:** the icon is not the target. The target is the tap-target minimum in 4.5 §5, with hit slop as needed.
*   **Meaning:** an icon is never the only carrier of meaning. Icon-only controls carry an accessible label describing the action.
*   **Set selection:** OPEN. It needs a set that ships as a vector component in Expo, has full weight consistency, and does not carry a heavy bundle cost. See §10.

### 6\. Imagery, Illustration & Texture

This app has almost no imagery, and that is correct. It is a tool, not a magazine.

*   **Photography:** only where the user supplies it — vision board, meals, personal inventory, adventures. The app never ships stock photography.
*   **User images are content, not decoration.** They get contained, not bled behind text.
*   **Illustration:** reserved for empty states, where one restrained mark is worth more than a paragraph. One illustration style, palette-limited, never cute.
*   **Texture:** `FUTURE-STATE` for Phase 4. Fine grain and layered translucency to give surfaces material depth.
*   **The texture rule:** texture sits BEHIND content and never under body text. If a texture changes the measured contrast of type sitting on it, the texture is wrong, not the type.
*   **Depth is built from borders, spacing and shadow, not from fill luminance.** See §3.5 — the palette cannot produce the luminance step, so stop asking it to.

| Do | Don't |
| ---| --- |
| Let user-supplied images be the only photography | Ship stock imagery to fill space |
| Keep illustration to empty states, palette-limited | Illustrate a screen the user visits daily |
| Put texture behind surfaces | Put texture behind readable text |
| Build depth with borders, spacing and shadow | Tint cards harder hoping they will separate |

### 7\. Spacing & Layout

*   **Base unit:** 4, with 8 as the working step. Full scale in 4.7 §4.
*   **Layout:** single column, 16 page gutter. No column grid on phone. Tablet and wide layouts are `FUTURE-STATE`.
*   **Density:** generous vertical spacing around anything tapped. Tight spacing is a desktop luxury; on a phone it produces mis-taps. See 4.5 §5.
*   **Rhythm:** related items sit close, unrelated groups sit apart. **On this palette, whitespace and hairlines do the grouping work that fill luminance cannot.** See §3.5.

### 8\. Motion

*   **Character: restrained. Motion confirms that an action landed. It does not entertain.**
*   The most important motion in this app is the feedback on a one-tap log — brief, certain, and over before the thumb moves.
*   Durations and easings are tokenized in 4.7 §8, so motion cannot drift into being decorative one component at a time.
*   **Rules:** nothing bounces. Nothing loops. No transition delays the user reaching a control. Sheets rise, screens push — the direction should match the mental model, not surprise it.
*   **Reduced motion is respected everywhere.** No component depends on animation to convey meaning; when motion is off, the meaning is still there in text or state.
*   Full specification in Motion (4.12).

### 9\. Overall Do's & Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
| ---| --- |
| Use Luminous Gold for the one thing that matters most on a screen | Spread accent colour across a screen until nothing leads |
| Write in Platinum, and write secondary text in Muted Platinum | Write in Terracotta, Rich Forest, Deep Amethyst or Midnight Forest |
| Set muted text as a REAL COLOUR VALUE | Make muted text by dropping Platinum's opacity until it looks right |
| Reserve Sunset Ember for things that went wrong | Use Ember for urgency, emphasis or energy |
| Give every warning an icon and a word | Rely on Gold-versus-Marigold hue difference to carry a state |
| Put Night text on a warm fill, Platinum text on a cool one | Choose the label colour on a fill by eye |
| Carry elevation on borders and spacing | Tint a card harder hoping it will float |
| Default to Urbanist until a display face is validated on device | Adopt the full four-font brand stack because the parent brand uses it |
| Let the tool look like the brand | Let the tool market the brand to its only user |
| Treat the 6am one-handed test as the acceptance test | Design for the screenshot |
